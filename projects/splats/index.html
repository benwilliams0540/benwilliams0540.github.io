Splats

Turn photos into 3D scenes.

Splats captures a subject from every angle and trains a Gaussian splat from the photos: millions of tiny, translucent ellipsoids that together reproduce the scene in 3D. Reconstruction and training run through ColmapKit and BrushKit, two Swift packages I built around the COLMAP and Brush engines.

Open source

ColmapKit↗

ColmapKit brings COLMAP's C++ reconstruction pipeline into Swift. I wrapped it in a C interface and ship it as an XCFramework, so an app can start a reconstruction, follow its progress, and cancel it without launching a command-line tool.

COLMAP project↗

BrushKit↗

BrushKit does the same for Brush, the Rust trainer that turns a reconstruction into a Gaussian splat. It embeds directly in Splats on Mac and iOS, with progress, cancellation, and training checkpoints available from Swift.

Brush project↗

Engineering

Capture as you move

Splats samples frames automatically as you move around a subject. Motion and camera-adjustment checks decide when a frame is saved, and each saved frame keeps its camera intrinsics and metadata.

Processing on devices you own

An iPhone or iPad can send a photo set to a paired Mac over the local network. Resumable transfers and shared job state keep both devices in step, and the finished scene returns to the capture device.

The full pipeline has also run entirely on an M1 iPad, though on-device creation is still experimental.

One viewer across Apple platforms

The Metal viewer opens PLY, SOG, SPLAT, and SPZ files directly, with orbit and fly controls for exploring a scene. Because rendering lives in its own module, the same renderer also powers Splatpaper, a live desktop wallpaper on Mac.
