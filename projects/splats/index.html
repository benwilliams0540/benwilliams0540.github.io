Splats

Viewer in public TestFlight

Capture, build, and explore Gaussian splats on iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

Splats combines guided camera capture, native reconstruction and splat training, paired-Mac compute, experimental on-device creation, and native multi-format Metal viewing in one local-first platform.

Gaussian splatting represents a scene with millions of small, view-dependent ellipsoids instead of a triangle mesh. Splats began as the native viewer I needed for my own scenes, then absorbed the capture, reconstruction, training, transfer, and progress workflow that used to surround it.

The public TestFlight contains Splats Viewer for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. Guided Capture and Factory are working product surfaces, but they are not included in that public TestFlight. On-device reconstruction and training are also experimental rather than public-release-ready.

Guided capture

Capture runs as a guided AVFoundation session. It locks focus, exposure, and white balance once the camera is ready, then uses quality and coverage guidance to help collect a useful photo set instead of an unstructured camera roll.

Each durable scene project preserves its original photos, per-frame intrinsics, capture metadata, and provenance. The guided customer loop stays simple: Capture, Review, Make, Progress, and Scene.

Compute targets

For heavier work, a capture device can hand the scene to a paired Mac. That path is proven end to end: the Mac reconstructs and trains locally, returns the finished scene, and both devices present one consistent job lifecycle.

Splats can also run reconstruction and training on the current device. The full photo-to-splat loop has completed on a physical M1 iPad, including a 60-photo foreground run, but the path remains experimental. That proof does not establish sustained thermals, background or relaunch recovery, representative quality, broad hardware support, or public-release readiness.

Capture-to-splat pipeline

Splats turns the workflow that once required several external tools into a guided product path. ColmapKit embeds COLMAP's sparse-reconstruction pipeline behind a native Apple framework, and BrushKit packages the Rust training engine behind a narrow C and Swift FFI boundary.

Capture data flows directly into those native engines and then into a scene that the viewer can open. There is no command-line handoff in the guided path.

Durable local-network orchestration

Bonjour pairing discovers a user-owned Mac on the local network. Verified, resumable chunked transfer carries the photos and metadata to it, while the capture device and Mac share progress through one durable cross-device lifecycle.

Scene photos and generated splats stay on devices you own. Splats does not upload them to a cloud compute service.

Viewer and public TestFlight

The native Metal viewer opens PLY, SOG, SPLAT, and SPZ scenes on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. It includes local files and recents, orbit and fly navigation, stable rendering, renderer tuning, and Splatpaper live wallpaper mode on macOS.

Splats Viewer is available through public TestFlight on all three platforms. Capture and Factory are not part of that public build yet. The live WebGL examples below use SOG versions of two real test scenes.

Native architecture

I keep product state, interface code, capture, workflow orchestration, engine integration, file loading, and rendering in separate Tuist-generated frameworks. TCA owns the guided state and behavior while SwiftUI composes the shared features for each Apple platform.
